Over the weekend, I was hiking with my Scout troop. By the time we reached the final stretch, we had covered nearly 10 miles. Everyone was tired when we came to a choice between two paths.

One route was a little easier. The other was significantly more challenging.

I turned to one of the Scouts who had been struggling most throughout the hike and asked which path he thought we should take.

Without hesitation, he replied:

> “We didn't walk this far to take the easy way.”

That simple statement stopped me in my tracks.

How often do we get near the end of a challenge and start looking for shortcuts? We invest months in a project, years in a career, or countless hours developing ourselves. Then, when the finish line is finally in sight, we feel tempted to settle.

The Scout carrying the heaviest burden understood something many adults forget: growth doesn't happen because the path is easy. It happens because we keep moving forward when it's hard.

In business, leadership, and life, meaningful achievements rarely come from choosing the easiest route. They come from having difficult conversations, solving tough problems, learning new skills, and pushing through the moments when quitting or cutting corners seems most appealing.

Perseverance isn't about how strong you are at the beginning. It's about the decision you make when you're tired, uncomfortable, and faced with a choice.

That lesson from the trail applies just as well in the boardroom, at work, and in life.
